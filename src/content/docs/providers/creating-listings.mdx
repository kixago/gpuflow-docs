Renters choose a GPU in a few seconds. A clear name, the right model and a short description that says which AI models you serve make the difference.

Open **Dashboard → List a GPU** (or click **List it for rent** on a machine that is online). The form looks like this:

<Image src={form} alt="The List a GPU form filled in with an example, each field numbered" />

## Field by field

**1. GPU Name (Your Listing Name).** What renters see first. Put the GPU and the main model in it.

> `RTX 4090 · qwen2.5 7B`
> `RTX 3060 12 GB · fast small models`
> `RTX 3090 · qwen2.5 14B, evenings and weekends`

**2. GPU Model (Official).** Pick your exact card from the list. It sets the VRAM shown on your listing and groups you with the same cards in the marketplace. You can't change it later.

**3. Hourly Rate (USD).** What a renter pays for one hour. See [how to price your GPU](/providers/pricing/) before you choose.

**4. Location.** Pick the nearest city from the list. Renters can filter the marketplace by location.

**5. Machine.** The computer that serves this listing. If you came from **List it for rent**, it's already chosen. Otherwise pick one that is online, or leave **None, attach one later** and use **Bring Online** on **My GPUs** afterwards.

**6. Key Features (comma-separated).** Short tags, separated by commas:

> `24 GB VRAM, qwen2.5:7b, llama3.1:8b, 1 Gbit upload`

**7. Description.** Two or three sentences. Say which models you serve, your connection and when the machine is usually on:

> RTX 4090 with 24 GB VRAM on a 1 Gbit line. Serves qwen2.5:7b and llama3.1:8b through an OpenAI-compatible API. Online most days from 08:00 to 23:00 (UTC-7).

> RTX 3060 with 12 GB VRAM on home fiber (500 Mbit). Serves qwen2.5:0.5b and qwen2.5:7b. Good for testing, small scripts and chat bots. Usually online 24/7.

Then click **List GPU**.

<Aside type="tip" title="Only promise what's true">
Write only the models that are actually installed on your machine, and only the hours you're really online. Renters who get what they expected come back.
</Aside>

## After you've listed

On **My GPUs** you can:

- **Edit:** change the name, price, location, description or the machine.
- **Unpublish:** hide the listing from the marketplace without deleting it. **Publish** brings it back.
- **Delete:** remove it for good. You can't delete a listing while it's rented.
- **Bring Online:** get the command for a listing whose machine is offline.

## What renters see

Your listing appears in the marketplace:

- in the green **Instant Access Available** section while your machine is online,
- in the yellow **Available - Provider Offline** section while it's off. Renters can click **Notify When Online** there. You get an email that someone is waiting, and they get one when you're back.

## Reviews and replies

After a rental, the renter can rate your GPU from 1 to 5 stars and leave a comment. The average and the number of reviews show on your listing page and on its card in the marketplace.

- You get a notification (the bell in the header) for every new review.
- Open your listing page. Under each review there is a **Reply publicly** button. Everyone can read your reply. You can change it later with **Edit reply**.

<Image src={reply} alt="A review on a listing page, with the review and the Reply publicly button highlighted" />

A short, calm reply works best, especially under a bad review. For example:

> Thanks for the feedback. The machine restarted for an update around 14:00, which is why the rental ended early. Updates now run at night.

You can't remove a review yourself. If one is abusive or fake, open a support ticket with a link to it.
