Your price is the single biggest reason a renter picks your GPU or someone else's. Here is what you keep, what it costs you to run, and what GPUs like yours rent for today.

## What you keep

You set an hourly price. The renter pays that price for the time they use. **GPUFlow keeps 12%, you get 88%.**

| Your price per hour | You earn per hour | 8 hours a day for 30 days |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $0.10 | $0.088 | $21.12 |
| $0.20 | $0.176 | $42.24 |
| $0.35 | $0.308 | $73.92 |
| $0.50 | $0.44 | $105.60 |

The last column assumes your GPU is rented 8 hours every day. Real use depends on your price, your model choice and how often you're online.

## What it costs you to run

The main cost is electricity. You can work it out in one minute:

<Steps>

1. Open **My Machines** while your GPU is busy. The card shows its **power draw** in watts.
2. Divide by 1,000 to get kilowatts. 350 W is 0.35 kW.
3. Multiply by your electricity price per kWh (it's on your power bill).

</Steps>

Example: 0.35 kW × $0.15 per kWh = **$0.053 per hour**. Your earnings per hour should be well above that.

<Aside type="note" title="Why start in the lower half">
On most of those sites a renter gets the whole machine. On GPUFlow they get your AI models through an API. That's simpler for them, but it can do less, so price yourself in the lower half of the range, at least until you have your first few rentals.
</Aside>

## The price bar on the listing form

When you type an hourly rate on **List a GPU** or **Edit**, a bar under the field shows where your price sits:

<Image src={bar} alt="The price bar under the Hourly Rate field, showing the price as typical for this GPU" />

- **Green band, "Other rental sites":** the usual range for your GPU model on other rental sites, from the table above.
- **Blue line, "Other GPUFlow listings of this model":** the average price of other GPUFlow listings with the same GPU model, with their lowest and highest price. It only shows when there are some.
- **Orange marker:** your price. The line under the bar says **Priced low**, **Typical price** or **Priced high**, and the last line tells you what you earn per hour after the 12% fee.

If your GPU isn't in the table and nobody else on GPUFlow lists it yet, the bar can't compare. Use the closest card in the table as a guide.

## Choosing your price: a simple plan

<Steps>

1. **Start low.** Pick a price in the lower third of the range for your GPU. For an RTX 4090 that's around $0.30 – $0.35.
2. **Stay online.** Renters can't rent an offline GPU. The hours you're online matter more than a few cents.
3. **Look again after a week.**
   - Rented most of the time? Raise the price by $0.02 – $0.05.
   - No rentals at all? Lower it by $0.02 – $0.05, and check that your description names your models.
4. **Check once a month.** Prices on other sites move. Change yours on **My GPUs → Edit**.

</Steps>
