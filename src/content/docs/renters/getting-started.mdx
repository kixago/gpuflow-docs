Renting a GPU on GPUFlow takes about five minutes. You need an email address and a payment card.

**What you get:** an API key for one GPU, for the hours you choose. The key works like an OpenAI key, so any app or script that talks to the OpenAI API can use it. There is no server to log into and nothing to install.

## 1. Create an account

<Steps>

1. Go to [gpuflow.app](https://gpuflow.app/en/join) and click **Sign Up** in the top right corner.
2. Fill in a username, your email address and a password (twice).
3. Click **Create Account**.

</Steps>

<Image src={signup} alt="The Create account form with the username, email and password fields and the Create Account button highlighted" />

You can also click **Continue with Google** or **Continue with GitHub** instead of making a password.

## 2. Confirm your email address

GPUFlow sends you an email right away. Open it and click the link inside.

<Aside type="caution">
You can't add credits until your email address is confirmed. If the email doesn't arrive within a few minutes, check your spam folder, or click **Resend email** on the yellow banner at the top of your dashboard.
</Aside>

## 3. Add credits

Credits are what you pay for rentals with. **1 credit = $0.01**, so $25 buys 2,500 credits.

<Steps>

1. Open your **Dashboard** and click **Payments** in the menu on the left.
2. Under **Add credits**, click an amount, or type your own. You can add from $10 to $500 at a time. There is no fee.
3. Click **Continue to payment**. You go to Stripe, our payment partner, to pay by card.
4. After paying you come back to GPUFlow. Your credits show up within a minute, at the top of the Payments page and on the orange button in the header.

</Steps>

<Image src={credits} alt="The Payments page: the amount buttons and the Continue to payment button highlighted" />

## 4. Pick a GPU

Click **Browse GPUs** at the top of any page. GPUs that are ready right now are in the green **Instant Access Available** section.

<Image src={market} alt="The marketplace with one GPU card and its Rent Now button highlighted" />

On each card, check:

- **The price per hour.** This is what one hour costs you.
- **VRAM.** Bigger AI models need more. As a rough guide, a 7B model runs well on 8 GB or more, a 14B model on 16 GB or more.
- **The model.** Click the GPU's name to open its page. Providers write which AI models they serve (for example `qwen2.5:7b`) in the description.

When you've picked one, click **Rent Now - Instant Access**.

## 5. Choose how many hours

<Steps>

1. Type the number of hours you want. Most GPUs allow 1 to 168 hours (one week). You can add more hours later.
2. Check the two lines under it: how much will be held from your credits, and how much you have.
3. Click the orange button that starts the rental. It shows your number of hours, for example **Start 2-hour rental**.

</Steps>

<Image src={rent} alt="The rental page with the Hours field and the start button highlighted" />

<Aside type="tip" title="You only pay for what you use">
The full amount for your hours is held when you start. When the rental ends, you pay only for the time you actually used, to the second, and the rest goes back to your credits. [How billing works](/renters/billing/).
</Aside>

## 6. Copy your API key

You land on **Current Rentals**. Your new rental is at the top.

<Image src={key} alt="Current Rentals: the API key box, the endpoint box and the Add hours, New key and End now buttons highlighted" />

1. **Your API key.** It starts with `gfk_`. Copy it now and keep it somewhere safe. **It is shown only once.**
2. **The endpoint:** `https://gpuflow.app/v1`. Use it as the "base URL" in your app.
3. **The buttons:** **Add hours** extends the rental, **New key** makes a new key and switches off the old one, **End now** stops the rental and returns the unused time to your credits.

Lost the key? Click **New key**. The old key stops working and a new one appears.

**Next:** [use your API key](/renters/api-quickstart/) with `curl`, Python, JavaScript or an app.

## If the GPU you want is offline

GPUs whose owner is offline are in the yellow **Available - Provider Offline** section. Click **Notify When Online** on the card.

<Image src={notify} alt="A GPU card in the offline section with the Notify When Online button highlighted" />

- You get an email and a notification (the bell in the header) as soon as that GPU comes online.
- The owner is told that someone is waiting for their GPU.
- Changed your mind? Click the button again (it now says **You'll be notified**) to stop.

## Rate your rental

When a rental ends, you can rate it. Your rating helps other renters choose, and it helps good providers get rented.

<Steps>

1. Open **Dashboard → Rental History**.
2. Find the rental and click **Rate this rental**.
3. Click 1 to 5 stars. A comment is optional.
4. Click **Post review**.

</Steps>

<Image src={rate} alt="The rating window with the stars, the comment box and the Post review button highlighted" />

A useful comment says what you used the GPU for and how it went. For example:

> Used it for about two hours with qwen2.5:7b. Fast answers and no drops. The models in the description were all there.

Good to know:

- You can rate a rental for 30 days after it ends, if it cost you something.
- You can change your review for 14 days after you post it. Click **Edit review** in **Rental History**.
- Your GPUFlow username is shown with your review. Your email address is not.
- The provider can reply publicly under your review. You get a notification when they do.
